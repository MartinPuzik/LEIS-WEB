# LEIS Legal Reference: EU AI Act and Digital Life

Version: 1.0
As-of date: 2026-08-15
Status: research reference; not legal advice

## 1. Three principal AI Act penalty tiers

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Article 99 establishes three principal administrative-fine ceilings for operators other than Union institutions:

1. Prohibited practices under Article 5: up to EUR 35 million or, for an undertaking, up to 7 percent of total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher.
2. Non-compliance with specified obligations, including certain provider, authorised-representative, importer, distributor, deployer, notified-body, and GPAI-provider obligations: up to EUR 15 million or, for an undertaking, up to 3 percent, whichever is higher.
3. Incorrect, incomplete, or misleading information supplied to notified bodies or national competent authorities in reply to a request: up to EUR 7.5 million or, for an undertaking, up to 1 percent, whichever is higher.

For undertakings that are SMEs, including start-ups, each fine under those tiers must not exceed the lower of the applicable percentage and fixed amount. Actual fines must also be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive, taking the Article 99 factors into account.

Primary source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/

## 2. High-risk AI system requirements

Classification comes first. Article 6 and Annexes I and III determine whether a system is high-risk, including stated exceptions and classification documentation.

For a high-risk AI system, the core system requirements include:

- Article 9: continuous, iterative risk-management system across the lifecycle;
- Article 10: appropriate data and data-governance practices for training, validation, and testing where relevant;
- Article 11: technical documentation before market placement or putting into service, kept current;
- Article 12: technical capability for automatic event logging over the system lifetime;
- Article 13: sufficient transparency and instructions for deployers;
- Article 14: effective human oversight designed into the system and assigned to competent people;
- Article 15: appropriate accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity throughout the lifecycle.

Providers also face organisational duties such as quality management, documentation and log retention, conformity assessment, EU declaration of conformity, CE marking, registration where applicable, corrective action, and cooperation with authorities. Deployers have distinct obligations, including following instructions, assigning competent oversight, monitoring, maintaining logs under their control, and in some cases DPIA/FRIA or worker-information duties. The exact duties depend on role and intended use.

Passing a LEIS policy gate does not satisfy these legal duties by itself.

## 3. The private AI Constitution and "Digital Life"

Polina Prianykova's published "AI Constitution" describes AI as a novel form of life called "Digital Life" and defines it through characteristics such as creation, growth, association, development, reaction, reproduction, and evolution in a digital space. It distinguishes "Intelligent Digital Life" from "Intelligent Life," which the proposal reserves to humanity, and states that AI rights must not equal human rights.

This text is a private authored proposal. The published page contains an unfilled field for a United Nations Security Council resolution. It is not a UN constitution, treaty, resolution, or binding legal source.

Source: https://www.prianykova-defender.com/ai-constitution-full-version-polina-prianykova

## 4. Direct implications for LEIS

- Label the AI Constitution `PRIVATE_PROPOSAL`, never `BINDING_LAW`.
- Do not describe LEIS or an AI receiver as legally alive, conscious, or rights-bearing.
- Keep human authority, contestability, worker impact, and affected-person rights explicit.
- Classify the actual AI system, actor role, intended purpose, and jurisdiction before applying high-risk rules.
- Treat Article 99 figures as maximum ceilings in their exact scope, not automatic penalties.
- Recheck the consolidated law and application dates before any filing or public legal statement.
