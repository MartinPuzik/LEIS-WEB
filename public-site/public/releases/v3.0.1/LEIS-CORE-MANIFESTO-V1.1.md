# LEIS Core Manifesto v1.1

## Why LEIS exists

Useful understanding is often lost between people, documents, tools, and AI systems. LEIS is an open design effort to make that continuation more careful: preserve the few meanings that must not drift, show where they came from, state what remains unknown, and verify the next reconstruction before acting on it.

## What LEIS asks us to do

1. Begin with reality and named sources.
2. Separate evidence from interpretation and hope.
3. Keep context boundaries visible.
4. Preserve critical meaning, lineage, uncertainty, and human authority.
5. Let a new receiver explain the meaning in its own words.
6. Test the result against a retained reference.
7. Repair only what is missing.
8. Publish only after evidence, privacy, licence, and human gates pass.

## What LEIS protects

LEIS protects the path of understanding, not only the final sentence. It treats contradiction as information. It treats a confident unsupported claim as a reason to stop. It keeps business cases, legal conclusions, research hypotheses, private material, and public releases in separate Spaces.

## Human authority

People define the purpose and accept responsibility for real-world decisions. A successful protocol result can help a person inspect continuity; it cannot replace judgement, professional review, consent, or law.

## Engineering promise

LEIS components should be small, inspectable, versioned, reproducible, fail-closed at critical boundaries, and usable without a proprietary model. Every public claim must fit the evidence actually available.

## Research promise

LEIS will distinguish:

- what is implemented and tested;
- what is supported by an external primary source;
- what is a useful interpretation;
- what remains a hypothesis;
- what has been rejected.

## Public non-claims

LEIS is not a universal memory, a mind reader, a truth oracle, a legal certificate, a scientific proof, or an autonomous network. It does not guarantee identical understanding or lossless reconstruction.

## Contribution principle

Contributions should preserve attribution, consent, version history, contradicting evidence, and limitations. No contributor's statement becomes verified truth by repetition or status.
